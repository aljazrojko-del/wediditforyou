## 3 — CONSOLIDATED MUST-FIX (deduped, ordered by leverage)

Each moves the needle. None blocks ship. Apply in v1 if time allows; ship regardless.

### Fix 1 — Close the dollarization in the cold money email *(highest leverage)*
**File:** `cold-email.md` (Email 3, line 198)
**FROM:**
> Owners without a website lose three to five jobs a week. Someone hears your name, goes to look you up, finds nothing, and calls the next guy. Not the better guy. The one with a website.

**TO:**
> Owners without a website lose three to five jobs a week. Call it one job at a hundred bucks and that's fifteen hundred a month walking to whoever shows up first. The site that stops it is four hundred fifty, once.

**WHY:** Never make the buyer do the multiplication you can do for him. "3-5 jobs a week" is a quantity; a broke-on-time owner needs the DOLLAR set physically next to $450 so the gap is undeniable. `nurture-react.md` LGN-2 already does exactly this ("Run your own number times five… it dwarfs the $450") — match it in the cold channel. (Note: rewording Email 3's cost line off the searcher mechanic also reduces the campaign-wide reliance on that one mold.)

### Fix 2 — Weaponize the recurring-bill contrast in cold E3
**File:** `cold-email.md` (Email 3, line 202)
**FROM:**
> A web shop charges three to four grand for this, then a monthly. The first ten owners get it for $450 once. After that it's $700, for good.

**TO:**
> A web shop charges three to four grand for this, then bills you every month after. This is yours for $450, once, nothing monthly, ever. First ten owners only, then it's $700.

**WHY:** This buyer's #1 fear is the recurring-bill trap. "Every month" vs "nothing monthly, ever" is a bigger no-brainer lever than the headline price gap. Attach the permanence ("ever") to the thing he actually fears, not to the $700.

### Fix 3 — Pull the 30-day job-or-refund guarantee into the cold sequence
**File:** `cold-email.md` (Email 3, beside the price, after line 202)
**ADD:**
> Take it 30 days. No paying job off it, I refund the $450 and you keep the site.

**WHY:** The campaign's most powerful risk-reversal is absent from the highest-skepticism channel, where it most violently answers "what's the catch." It already lives correctly in post-call/booking/post-sale — it is simply missing where skepticism is highest.

### Fix 4 — Break the searcher-skeleton repeat in post-call Lane A *(+ correct the false audit)*
**File:** `post-call.md` (E3 Variation A, line 156)
**ACTION:** Reword E3-A's cost line OFF the searcher mechanic — use peer-status or the lived quiet-week angle — since E2-A (line 117) already owns "they search you, find nothing, call the other guy." A Lane-A reader (E1-A → E4-A congruent) currently hears the identical mold in BOTH E2-A and E3-A.
**ALSO:** Correct the pre-flight table (line 315) which falsely claims the searcher skeleton "appears ONCE in this workflow (E2)." It appears in E2-A and E3-A. The green checkmark is wrong; fix the copy and the audit line.

### Fix 5 — Break the searcher-skeleton repeat in inbound Piece 4a
**File:** `inbound-form.md` (Piece 4 Variation a, line 254)
**ACTION:** Reword Piece 4a's cost line to peer-status or the lived quiet-week angle so the a-with-a paired journey doesn't hear the identical loss-mold twice. Piece 3a (line 194) already owns the searcher mechanic; for the warmest, most valuable channel, hearing the same skeleton twice reads as a template — the exact tell the de-patterning pass exists to kill. (3b at line 213 also carries it, but 3b/4a don't pair.)

### Fix 6 — Stack the EV on the post-call guarantee
**File:** `post-call.md` (E3 Variation A, line 156)
**FROM:**
> Then give it 30 days. If you don't get at least one paying job off it, I refund the $450 and you keep the site anyway.

**TO:**
> Land one job in 30 days and the site already paid for itself twice over. Don't, and I refund the $450 and you keep the site anyway.

**WHY:** The guarantee already reverses risk; the rewrite reverses risk AND makes the EV obviously positive. For a money-anxious owner, feeling that ONE job (worth more than $450) makes the refund clause almost impossible to lose on is the difference between "safe" and "insane to refuse."
