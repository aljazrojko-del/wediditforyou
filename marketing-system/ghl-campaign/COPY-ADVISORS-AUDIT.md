# WDIFY — COPY ADVISORS AUDIT (adversarial, line-level)

**Date:** 2026-06-28 · **Auditor panel:** Hormozi · Halbert/Schwartz · Ogilvy/Sugarman · Voss · Shleyner · cold-email deliverability · AI-detection adversary
**Files audited:** `cold-email.md` · `inbound-form.md` · `post-call.md` · `booking.md` · `nurture-react.md` · `postsale.md`
**Bar:** "best cold-traffic copy on the internet" for a busy, skeptical, often-burned US blue-collar mobile-service owner who reads on a phone, between jobs, days late. Not "good." Best.

---

## HOW I SCORED

Two axes, 0–10 each:
- **(a) Human / zero-AI-detection** — would GPTZero/Originality flag it? Would a sharp blue-collar owner smell a bot? Penalizes: uniform rhythm, parallel tricolons, AI-tell vocabulary, "It seems like" openers, formula smell, every-line-same-length cadence.
- **(b) Conversion power** — Hormozi cost-of-inaction, Voss calibration, slippery-slope flow, does saying no feel insane, does it earn the reply/booking from THIS buyer.

The campaign is already **well above average**. The authors clearly internalized the copy law — past-tense gift, walk-aways, no begging, device rotation, em-dash stripping. So this audit is graded against the *ceiling*, not the floor. Where I'm hard, it's because the line is 80th-percentile and the brief demands 99th.

# FILE 1 — `cold-email.md` (4-touch cold, subject-only A/B)

### Scores
- **(a) Human / zero-AI-detection: 8.5/10**
- **(b) Conversion power: 9/10**

This is the strongest file in the set. The past-tense gift ("I already built {{company}} a website") is a genuine pattern-break and the single best asset in the whole campaign. Zero-link Email 1, the device rotation, the real walk-away with no Email 5 — all correct. It reads like a person.

### Weakest lines + exact rewrites

**1. Email 1 — "built from your reviews and the work you actually do."**
> The phrase "the work you actually do" is filler emphasis — "actually" is a tell that the writer is leaning on intensifiers instead of specifics. A blue-collar owner doesn't say "the work you actually do."
**Rewrite:** `built from your reviews and what you fix.` (or the trade noun: "...and the jobs you run"). Concrete verb beats the vague "work you actually do."

**2. Email 3 — "Someone hears your name, can't find you, and hires whoever they can. Not whoever's better. Whoever they can find."**
> The "Not whoever's better. Whoever they can find." fragment-pair is a *known copywriting move* (the deliberate sentence-fragment echo). It's effective once, but it's the kind of constructed rhythm a detector clusters on, and it's the third time the campaign uses the "books the guy who shows up" idea. Strong but slightly writerly.
**Rewrite (keep the sting, lower the polish):** `Someone hears your name, goes to look you up, finds nothing, and calls the next guy. Not the better guy. The one with a website.` — "the one with a website" lands the mechanism plainer than "whoever they can find."

**3. Email 3 — "Would it be a bad idea to grab one before the price moves?"**
> This exact no-oriented stem ("Would it be a bad idea to...") appears in inbound-form, nurture-react REA-1B, booking REVIEW-1B, and postsale. **It is the single most over-used construction in the entire campaign.** Across files a reader could plausibly see it 2–3 times. It's the #1 AI/formula fingerprint here.
**Rewrite:** `Want me to hold one of the ten for you?` — still autonomy-preserving, but a direct offer, not the fourth "would it be a bad idea" in the campaign.

**Verdict:** Closest to the bar. Tighten the over-used Voss stem and the one writerly fragment-echo and it's a 9.5.

# FILE 2 — `inbound-form.md` (Entry B warm form, 4 pieces × a/b × email+SMS)

### Scores
- **(a) Human / zero-AI-detection: 6.5/10**
- **(b) Conversion power: 8/10**

The warmest channel, and the copy is solid — but this is where the **"It seems like..."** opener lives, twice, back to back across Piece 3 a and b. That is the exact construction the copy law and every AI-detector flag. The file's own self-audit even lists "It seems like" as an approved Voss label. It is not. It's the most-flagged AI sentence-opener on the internet.

### Weakest lines + exact rewrites

**1. Piece 3a — "It seems like you looked and weren't sure what comes next."**
> "It seems like" is the textbook GPT hedge-opener. A blue-collar owner never starts a sentence this way. It's a label, yes — but the *grammar* is the tell, not the function.
**Rewrite:** `My guess is you opened it, then the day swallowed you before you decided anything.` — same label (mirrors his unspoken state), zero AI-opener, sounds like a man talking.

**2. Piece 3b — "It seems like the only thing between you and done is fifteen minutes."**
> Second "It seems like" in adjacent variations. Even split-tested, it's a campaign-wide tic.
**Rewrite:** `You're fifteen minutes from done. That's the whole gap.` — flatter, harder, human.

**3. Piece 1a — "You're probably wondering what the catch is."**
> This is a *good* accusation audit, but "You're probably wondering..." is itself a slightly worn opener and it shares DNA with "You probably get pitches" (cold-email) and "You wanted to know if this was real" (postsale). The "you're probably [verb]ing" frame recurs.
**Rewrite:** `Here's the catch you're looking for: there isn't one, and the next email proves it.` — names the catch-hunt without the "you're probably wondering" stem, and resolves with proof (which the WHY note says is the goal anyway).

**4. Piece 2a — "Opposed to grabbing a time today?"**
> "Opposed to X?" is a Voss no-oriented question, correct — but it's terse to the point of reading clipped/templated, and the campaign caps "Opposed to / would it be a bad idea" at ≤2 per journey (its own rule), which this is straining against.
**Rewrite:** `Bad idea to grab fifteen minutes today and finish it?` — warmer, still a no-question, less robotic than the bare "Opposed to."

**Verdict:** Kill both "It seems like" openers and this jumps to ~8.5 human. They are the worst AI-tell in the campaign and they're sitting in the warmest channel.

# FILE 3 — `post-call.md` (4-email post-connect + SMS twins)

### Scores
- **(a) Human / zero-AI-detection: 8/10**
- **(b) Conversion power: 8.5/10**

Strong. Email 1A's "You half-expected I'd never actually send it" is excellent — that's a real human reading the prospect's mind. The walk-aways are dignified. The one real problem is **Email 3A runs ~139 words** and is doing five jobs at once (rough-cut + guarantee + agency anchor + effort-collapse + dream). It's defensible as the money email, but it's the one place the copy stops sounding like a text from Alex and starts sounding like a landing page.

### Weakest lines + exact rewrites

**1. Email 3A — "A web shop charges three to four grand for this, then a monthly. Yours is $450 once."**
> The anchor is right (Hormozi would keep it). But stacked on top of the guarantee + the dream + the rough-cut frame, the email loses the slippery slope — each sentence stops earning the next because there are too many of them. Ogilvy: every line must pull the eye to the next; by sentence 7 the reader's eye is tired.
**Rewrite (cut, don't add):** Drop the "A web shop charges three to four grand" line from E3A entirely — it already lives in cold-email E3 and booking 2A. Let E3A be: rough cut → 15 min is the whole job → locks $450 → 30-day guarantee → the first customer this week. Five beats, not seven. The anchor isn't needed at the post-call stage; they already heard price on the call.

**2. Email 2A — "Did the day get away from you before you had a real look? Happens."**
> "Happens." as a one-word sentence is a writerly beat — effective, but it's the second "[fragment]. [One word]." rhythm in the campaign (cold-email's "That's it." family). Fine in isolation; watch the cumulative tic.
**Keep, but** if E1 or another touch in the same lane uses the one-word-sentence beat, vary this to: `Did the day get away before you had a real look? It always does.`

**3. Email 4B — "The founding price won't wait forever, and neither will the spot."**
> "won't wait forever" is the softest, most generic line in the file — it's the kind of phrase that could appear in any SaaS email. Vague scarcity, not the specific founding-ten mechanic the rest of the campaign earns.
**Rewrite:** `Ten founding spots. When they're gone the price is $700, and that's that.` — concrete, matches the real mechanic, no "forever."

**Verdict:** Cut E3A back to five beats and this is a 9. The over-stuffed money email is the only structural weakness.

# FILE 4 — `booking.md` (confirmation → reminders → no-show → reschedule)

### Scores
- **(a) Human / zero-AI-detection: 7.5/10**
- **(b) Conversion power: 8.5/10**

The strategic spine is genuinely excellent — protecting show rate is the right obsession, and "Miss it and it stays a draft" is a perfect loss-line. But this file has the **most rule-of-three rhythm** in the campaign (the "same... same... same" SMS, the tricolons), and tricolons are an AI-detector magnet.

### Weakest lines + exact rewrites

**1. 5.2-SMS-B — "Same 15 min, same $450, same finish line."**
> A deliberate rule-of-three. Reads designed. A busy owner texting between jobs doesn't write parallel triplets; detectors love them.
**Rewrite:** `Nothing changed but the time. Still 15 min, still $450.` — breaks the triplet, keeps the reassurance.

**2. 1A-Email-A — "Your real number goes in, your own photos go up, and we show you exactly what a customer sees the moment they look you up."**
> "your real number goes in, your own photos go up" is a parallel-structure pair feeding a long third clause. The "your X, your Y" rhythm is flagged in the campaign's own notes as a "campaign-wide tic" — and here it is again, uncut.
**Rewrite:** `Your number and your photos go in, and you see exactly what a customer sees when they look you up.` — one clause for the inputs, drops the parallel-verb construction.

**3. 2A (24h reminder B) — "Every week it isn't, somebody who'd have called you calls someone else instead. They couldn't find you to begin with."**
> This is a *restructured* version of the banned searcher-skeleton, and the restructure made it clunkier than the original. "somebody who'd have called you calls someone else instead" trips over itself.
**Rewrite:** `Every week it isn't live, the calls that had your name on them go to someone they could actually find.` — cleaner, same cost-of-inaction, reads in one breath.

**4. 4B-Email-A subject — "have you given up"**
> Subject line. It's a no-oriented question (good), but "have you given up" is mildly accusatory/deflating for a no-show recovery whose whole doctrine is "zero shame." It risks the exact feeling the body works to avoid.
**Rewrite:** `still want this` — lighter, still pulls the "yeah, I do" reflex, no shame.

**Verdict:** Break the triplets and the parallel-verb pairs. The strategy is 9.5; the surface rhythm drags it to 7.5 on the human axis.

# FILE 5 — `nurture-react.md` (long-game nurture + reactivation)

### Scores
- **(a) Human / zero-AI-detection: 7/10**
- **(b) Conversion power: 8.5/10**

The mechanism (slow cadence, two pressure axes, possession-forward) is smart and the live-counter scarcity fix (`spots_remaining` instead of "almost gone") is exactly right — that's the one place the campaign got *more* honest and it shows. But this is the longest file and the cumulative load of "still live / still yours / still finishable" parallelism is heavy, and **REA-1A "Here's the part that stings"** echoes a construction used elsewhere.

### Weakest lines + exact rewrites

**1. REA-1A — "Here's the part that stings. Every week since, the customer who searched your name found nothing and hired the guy whose site loaded."**
> "Here's the part that stings" + the full searcher-skeleton. The skeleton is supposed to be capped at one instance per workflow, and the "Here's the part that [verb]s" stem ("Here's the honest part" in post-call E3A, "Here's the catch" suggested above) is becoming a campaign signature. Two tells in one line.
**Rewrite:** `And here's what it cost you: every week since, the guy whose site loaded got the customer who went looking for you.` — same blow, drops "the part that stings," tightens the skeleton.

**2. REA-1A — "still up. You still own it. It still hasn't cost you a thing"**
> Three "still" in three short sentences. The triple-anaphora ("still... still... still") is the single most detector-flaggable rhythm in the file. It's also in the Mia brief and REA-2.
**Rewrite:** `still up, and it still hasn't cost you a dime. You own it.` — one "still" pair, then a flat ownership statement. Anaphora broken.

**3. LGN-2A — "Mobile businesses without a real site lose three to five of those a week to whoever a customer can actually find instead."**
> "whoever a customer can actually find" — "actually" again (the same intensifier tell flagged in cold-email), and "Mobile businesses without a real site" is a category-statistic opener that reads like marketing, not Alex.
**Rewrite:** `A guy in your trade with no real site loses three to five of those a week to whoever shows up when someone looks.` — "a guy in your trade" is personal; drops "actually."

**4. REA-2A — "But that was 60-odd days ago, and that's two months of someone else picking up the calls that had your name on them."**
> Good line, but "60-odd days ago" then "two months" says the same span twice in one sentence — redundancy a sharp reader notices.
**Rewrite:** `That was two months ago. Two months of someone else picking up the calls that had your name on them.` — the repetition of "two months" is now intentional and rhetorical, not accidental.

**Verdict:** Break the "still/still/still" anaphora and retire "Here's the part that stings." The honesty upgrade on scarcity is the campaign's best single fix; don't let the rhythm undercut it. ~8 human after the cuts.

# FILE 6 — `postsale.md` (payment → onboarding → review)

### Scores
- **(a) Human / zero-AI-detection: 7.5/10**
- **(b) Conversion power: 8/10**

Post-sale, so the persuasion bar is lower (they already bought) — the job is reassurance and the review ask, and it mostly nails both. REVIEW-1A "the site you almost didn't believe was real" is the best line in the file. But the **payment emails run long and stack fragments**, and the review follow-ups lean on the same "next guy like you" frame three times.

### Weakest lines + exact rewrites

**1. PAY-1A — "Charged $450, one time. That's it. The founding price, locked, before it goes to $700 for everyone after you."**
> Three fragments in a row ("Charged $450, one time." / "That's it." / "The founding price, locked,"). The staccato fragment-burst is the file's signature and it's the most constructed-sounding rhythm here. The file even names it ("fragment-burst skeleton") — naming a tic doesn't make it not a tic.
**Rewrite:** `Your card was charged $450, one time, and that's the whole bill. You locked the founding price before it jumps to $700.` — two clean sentences, reads like a receipt a human wrote.

**2. PAY-1B — "You wanted to know if this was real before you paid a stranger. Here's your answer:"**
> Good accusation audit, but "Here's your answer:" is a colon-and-payoff construction that recurs ("Here's the catch", "Here's the part that stings", "Here's exactly how"). The "Here's [the X]:" stem is a campaign-wide signature now.
**Rewrite:** `You wanted to know this was real before paying a stranger. It just went through, no deposit, and nothing else ever comes out.` — drops the "here's your answer" hinge, states the proof directly.

**3. WELCOME-1A — "Your photos, your number, your colors. I'm putting those in now"**
> The "your X, your Y, your Z" tricolon — explicitly flagged as a campaign tic in other files, un-cut here.
**Rewrite:** `I'm putting your photos, number, and colors in now, off the notes from our call.` — same content, no tricolon-fragment.

**4. REVIEW-2A & B both lean on "the next mobile owner / the next guy" —** by the third review touch it's a formula.
> REVIEW-1 used it, REVIEW-2A uses it, REVIEW-2B uses it. The solidarity frame is strong but it's now the only note the review sequence plays.
**Rewrite for REVIEW-2B:** swap the "next guy" angle for the owner's own pride: `You took a cold call and it turned into a paying job. Thirty seconds saying that is worth more than anything I could write: {{review_link}}`

**Verdict:** De-fragment the payment emails, vary the review frame. ~8.5 human after.

# OVERALL VERDICT

**Is this messaging genuinely zero-AI-slop, deeply humanized, and super-converting? — Not yet. It's close (~80th–90th percentile), and the strategy is championship-grade, but it is NOT yet "best cold-traffic copy on the internet."**

What's championship-grade already:
- The **past-tense gift** ("I already built you a website") — the single best strategic asset, correctly load-bearing across every file.
- **Real walk-aways** with no fake "last chance #4." Genuine, and rare.
- **Live-counter scarcity** (`spots_remaining`) replacing "almost gone" — an honesty upgrade most marketers never make.
- **Concrete cashable guarantee** ("at least one paying job in 30 days") replacing weasel "obvious choice."
- **No begging.** The copy law held — zero "just checking in," zero "I'd love."

What keeps it off the top shelf — three campaign-wide fingerprints a detector AND a sharp owner will catch:
1. **The "would it be a bad idea / opposed to" no-question stem is over-used** across 4+ files. Voss devices must vary; this one became the house style.
2. **"It seems like" openers survive in inbound-form** — the most-flagged AI sentence-opener in existence, sitting in the warmest channel.
3. **Parallel rhythm is everywhere:** "your X, your Y, your Z" tricolons, "still/still/still" anaphora, "same/same/same" triplets, fragment-bursts. Individually fine; cumulatively they are the texture an AI-detector scores on, and the campaign's own notes admit these are "tics" — then ship them anyway.

The fix is not a rewrite. It's a **de-patterning pass**: vary the Voss stems, kill the two "It seems like," and break every deliberate triplet/anaphora into uneven human rhythm. That single pass takes the campaign from 8 to 9.5.
