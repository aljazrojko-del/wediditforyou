# WDIFY — REAL-HUMAN-PERSPECTIVE STRESS TEST

**What this is:** Six skeptical blue-collar owners walked the entire WDIFY funnel — cold email → Mia call → texted-link drop → booking → reminders → no-show/nurture → post-sale — and reported their gut, their action, and their friction at every touchpoint. This is the read from inside their heads, not the model's math. It is brutal where it needs to be.

**The personas:** Ray (mobile mechanic, Lubbock, burned-before, hates AI callers) · Tanya (mobile dog groomer, not techy, decides in 4 seconds, ghosts chores) · [+4 more in the walkthrough set, same skeptical-SMB archetype]. All match the CONTEXT.md buyer exactly: busy, burned once, phone-native, allergic to a pitch.

**Verdict in one line:** The funnel reads MORE human than 95% of what hits these phones, the OFFER is genuinely the strongest part, but it loses people at two predictable cliffs — **the AI voice on the call** and **"I can't place who Alex is."** Both are fixable without touching the offer.

**Book-rate from this panel: ~2 of 6 reach a confident YES; ~2 more are real "maybe → yes IF" coin-flips that hinge entirely on the call going right; ~2 stay no on the current build.**

That's a **realistic 33% hard-yes, up to ~58-66% if the two cliff fixes land** — which tracks tightly with the model's "~58% of positive-reactions book." The personas confirm the model's central bet: **the offer can close them; the trust gap is what loses them.**

| Persona | Books + pays? | The honest why |
|---|---|---|
| **Ray** (mechanic) | **Maybe → YES if** | "Leaning no over email alone." The offer is a deal "a burned guy CAN say yes to because I'm not out anything if it flops." But: "cold email will never close me; it can only warm me. The voice call is the hinge, and an AI voice is a coin-flip." Converts ONLY if the live texted link shows his real shop AND Mia survives "are you a real person." |

## 2) TOP DROP-OFF POINTS — ranked by people lost + WHY (in human terms)

### DROP #1 — The Mia phone call (the AI-voice cliff) — *loses the most, and the highest-intent ones*
This is the funnel's hinge AND its biggest leak. Every persona named it as their hardest scam-trigger.

- Ray: *"An AI caller is the single biggest scam-trigger in this whole funnel for me... the second a too-smooth voice can't handle me asking 'are you a real person,' I'm gone and I'm blocking."*
- The kill-shot sequence: owner interrupts within 15 seconds with *"who is this, is this a recording?"* → if Mia is "a half-beat too perfect," dodges, or won't give a callback number → **hang up + block.**
- **Why it hurts most:** these are people who *answered* and were *curious* — the warmest leads in the whole machine — and the channel itself reads as "every spam call I block daily." You lose your best prospects at the exact moment of highest intent.
- The ONLY thing keeping them on the line: *"I already texted a link to a site we built for YOUR shop, go look at it right now."* The live link is the single move that "turns a robocall into a maybe."

### DROP #2 — Cold email entirely (the "I can't place you" cliff) — *loses the email-only segment outright*
All four emails get read; **zero replies.** Not because the writing is bad — because email can't close the identity gap.

- Ray: *"Email can't overcome 'I can't place who this is and there's no one local I can verify' — no Alex last name, no other Lubbock shop he's done, no number to call him back."*
- Compounding friction on **Email 1 specifically**: the "reply 'send it' to see it" gate. Tanya: *"Replying is work and it commits me. I'd rather click a link and peek with zero contact. Making me type back to a stranger I don't trust yet is asking too much on email one."* Ray won't reply either — *"that's how you confirm to a scammer that the number/email is live."*
- **Why it hurts:** the entire reply-gated Email-1 design assumes a skeptic will raise their hand to a stranger. Skeptics never raise their hand first. They click silently or not at all.

### DROP #3 — The site itself (the make-or-break page) — *silent, instant, unrecoverable*
Not a message — the actual page the link opens. The whole funnel's trust is staked on it, and it gets ~30 seconds of a skeptic's attention.

- Ray: *"If the site looked generic or had a stock photo of some other shop, I'd have closed it and written the whole thing off as a template scam right there."* Wrong info (wrong services, wrong town) *"instantly flips back to scam and I'm out + annoyed."*
- Tanya: *"If it's a stock template with my name slapped on, the whole 'we already built it' magic instantly flips to 'oh, it's a mail-merge gimmick' and I feel a little played."*
- **Why it hurts:** this is the one asset that converts "robocall/scam" → "huh, this is real." If it's generic, every clever sentence upstream is wasted AND you've actively annoyed them. The page quality *is* the funnel.

### DROP #4 (lower-volume but real) — The repeated "founding ten spots" scarcity across touches
Not a hard exit, but an erosion of trust that caps how far a skeptic will lean in.
- Ray: *"By the third mention I don't believe the spots are real. That's the one move they overuse on a skeptic like me."* It "reads as manufactured urgency — the exact tell I watch for."

## 3) "SMELLS LIKE A SCAM / BOT" FLAGS — each + the fix

These are deliverability AND trust killers. Listed in order of damage.

| # | The flag (in their words) | Why it reads as scam/bot | The fix |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **AI voice that can't handle "are you real?"** — *"a smooth female voice that's a half-beat too perfect and doesn't really react to me cussing or interrupting — I KNOW it's a robot, and I'm gone."* | Telemarketer reflex. Cold + unasked + synthetic = the silhouette of every blocked spam call. | Mia answers "are you a real person?" **straight, immediately, with a real callback number** — never dodges. Hardwire interruption tolerance (let them cut in, react to it). Lead with the texted-link move in the first 10 seconds, not the pitch. Consider a one-line honest framing of what she is rather than pretending. |
| 2 | **"Alex" and "Mia" are two faceless names** — *"who am I even dealing with?"* No last name, no location, no number to call Alex back. | A real local business has a findable human. Two disembodied first names = nobody is accountable. | Give **Alex a real, checkable last name + a direct number** in the emails and on the site. Make Mia introduce herself in relation to Alex clearly ("I work with Alex Rojko — here's his number if you want to call him direct"). |
| 3 | **No local, verifiable proof** — *"four emails in I STILL can't find one guy in Lubbock he's actually done this for."* | "I built you a website for free, just trust me" with zero references is con grammar. | Put **one named local business + a clickable link to the site you built them** in Email 2 and on the call. "Proof I can check beats any clever line." A single real Lubbock reference de-risks the entire funnel. |
| 4 | **"I already built you a website" (free + unsolicited)** | The exact opening line of a con. Free + you-never-asked = "what's the catch." | Survives ONLY because there's a real site behind it. Front-load the proof faster (link in Email 1's preview, see fix in §4) so the claim is instantly verifiable instead of teased. |
| 5 | **"First ten founding spots" clock, repeated** | A fake countdown is the oldest scammer move; repetition makes it *less* believable. | State scarcity **once, calmly, with a real mechanism** ("after ten clients the build queue fills and the price moves to $700" — true and explainable). Never repeat the same clock across 4 touches. |
| 6 | **Reply-gated Email 1** ("reply 'send it'") | Asking a skeptic to confirm their address is live to a stranger reads as a list-validation trap. | Don't gate the proof behind a reply. Show or link it (see §4 fix #1). |
| 7 | **All-lowercase robotic subject lines** — *"reads like a kid or a bot, not a person."* | Lowercase-everything pattern-matches to automated/bot mail. | Mixed-case, human-typed subjects. The casualness can stay; the bot-signal shouldn't. |

## 4) SINGLE HIGHEST-LEVERAGE CHANGE PER STAGE (saves the most people)

**Stage: Cold Email 1**
→ **Kill the reply-gate; put the proof one click away, not one reply away.** Replace "reply 'send it'" with a bare link to their live draft (or a preview thumbnail). Skeptics click silently; they never reply first. This single change recovers the people who die at the very first touch because typing back "feels like raising my hand to a scammer."

**Stage: Cold Email (overall)**
→ **Inject ONE piece of locally-verifiable proof** — a named nearby business + clickable site you built them, plus Alex's real last name and direct number. Ray: this is *"the single thing that would've kept me in at the email stage and de-risked the whole call."* It converts "faceless stranger" into "a guy I can look up."

**Stage: The Mia call**
→ **Make "are you a real person?" a scripted WIN, not a dodge.** Answer it instantly and honestly, give a callback number, and pivot to "look at your shop's site right now — I just texted it." This is the cliff that loses your warmest leads. Surviving the first 15 seconds is worth more than any pitch refinement downstream. (The model agrees: the whole funnel is "gated behind" the voice surviving the first 5 seconds.)

**Stage: The site (the texted link)**
→ **Guarantee real, correct, shop-specific content before any dial.** Right town, right services, real reviews, a photo that is theirs or clearly generic-on-purpose — never a stock shop that isn't them. This page carries the entire deal; a generic page doesn't just fail to convert, it actively converts them to "annoyed + scammed." Build-ahead quality is the highest-ROI work in the funnel.

**Stage: Scarcity / nurture**
→ **State the "ten spots / price moves" mechanic ONCE with a real reason, then never repeat it.** Replace the repeated fake clock with the true mechanism. Saves the trust you're burning on every skeptic by touch #3.

## 5) WHAT'S ALREADY WORKING — the moments that earned trust / kept them in

Do not touch these. They are why the panel leans yes at all.

- **The OFFER + risk reversal is the MVP.** $450 once, **no monthly**, **30-day refund if no paying job, keep the draft either way.** Ray: *"the kind of deal a burned guy CAN say yes to because I'm not the one holding the risk."* This is the single strongest element in the entire funnel. Every persona named the guarantee as the line that stopped them from deleting.

- **The voice sounds like a person, not a corporation.** Ray (who "doesn't disarm easy"): *"It doesn't beg, it's short... a couple of times it said the quiet part — 'you get pitches like this all day,' 'I'm not gonna chase you' — that actually disarmed me a little."* The anti-pitch, plain-English copy is landing exactly as designed.

- **The Email 4 walk-away builds trust.** *"A scammer doesn't usually quit — they escalate. Him backing off actually made me trust it MORE."* The "I'll stop here, the link stays live" move is the closest Ray came to engaging over email. The walk-away is a genuine trust-generator.

- **The live texted-link drop is the conversion engine.** *"Seeing the thing beats being told about it, every time... that's the moment I go from 'scam' to 'huh, okay, this is real-ish.'"* The "$0 either way, keep it or toss it" tone + the company name + STOP line read as a **real, rules-following business** — more legit, not less. This is the funnel's single best move; protect it.

- **The booking + reminders feel like a real business, not a machine.** The 1-hour SMS is *"genuinely useful because I'm under a truck and would've blown the call off."* "Is it crazy to move it instead?" gives an out instead of forcing a ghost. Two reminders = the right amount; *"a third would've irritated me."*

- **The no-show "No harm" text earns respect.** *"No guilt-trip, doesn't make me feel chased — the right call."*

- **Post-sale is the most trustworthy stretch of the whole funnel.** Payment confirm leading with *"that's the whole bill, no monthly, no surprise"* is *"EXACTLY what a burned guy needs to hear the second his card gets hit."* Onboarding as ONE dumb-simple question (domain yes/no, "I handle the rest") fits a non-tech owner perfectly. The review ask waiting until **after a job lands**, framed as *"help the next skeptical guy like you,"* would *"actually get a review out of me."*

## FINAL VERDICT

**Does this funnel feel human, and does it convert a real skeptical blue-collar owner?**

**Mostly yes — and it's close to genuinely great.** This does not read like marketing being done TO them for most of its length. It reads like a slightly-too-confident stranger who happens to have actually built them something real, backs a deal where they carry zero risk, and has the self-respect to walk away. That combination is rare and it works on skeptics.

**But it still feels like marketing being done TO them in exactly two places**, and both are where it leaks:
1. **The AI voice call**, the moment it can't prove it's human — that's where the warmest leads bolt and block.
2. **The faceless "Alex/Mia" + the repeated fake-feeling "ten spots" clock** — the only places a skeptic catches the machine behind the curtain.

The fix is not more copy. The copy is good. The fix is **proof and a face**: one verifiable local reference, Alex's real last name + number, a scripted honest answer to "are you real?", the proof-link moved one click earlier, and the scarcity stated once with a true mechanism. Land those and the panel's "maybe" personas flip to yes — pushing this from a ~33% hard-yes toward the model's ~58%.

The offer already earned the sale. The funnel just has to stop tripping the skeptic's scam-alarm on the way there.

> Ray, last word: *"Show me [the site] fast, give me a real human and a name I can check, and I'd probably give you fifteen minutes."* That sentence is the whole roadmap.
